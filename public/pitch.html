HOLD MY PLACE · IEEE HACK FOR HUMANITY · TRACK 2

A pause shouldn’t
mean starting over.

A private browser memory aid that keeps one clear next step for your return.

Naveen Chatlapalli · Live prototype · Recorded demo

01 / THE NEED

The tab is still there.
The intention isn’t.

For people with attention, memory or executive-function difficulties, returning from an interruption can require reconstructing an entire task.

W3C cognitive-accessibility guidance and task-interruption research motivate the design. Direct participant validation is next.

02 / THE EXPERIENCE

Hold. Step away.
Find your way back.

Alt+H captures labels and fill states. A short cue appears when you return. Continue focuses your next incomplete field.

Read, hear, edit or delete it. No autofilling. No automatic submission.

03 / THE BUILD

Local-first, with optional AI.

Plain JavaScript MV3 extension → chrome.storage.local → return banner.

User-reviewed metadata → Cloudflare Worker → Workers AI → a bounded cue. Static Assets/CDN hosts the demo; a rate-limit binding protects inference.

No account or cloud database. A local rule-based fallback works offline.

04 / TRUST & EVIDENCE

Useful support.
Honest limits.

Typed answers never enter saved captures. AI is opt-in; the exact payload is visible. Sensitive-page guards and check-first cues reduce risky suggestions.

Installed-extension tests cover capture, privacy, navigation, correction and wiping. Full assistive-technology testing and participant outcomes remain unverified.

05 / WHAT COMES NEXT

Measure the return
to independence.

Counterbalanced study: 5–8 volunteers, comparable mock tasks, five-minute interruptions. Measure completion, time to resume, re-reading, duplicate actions, effort and cue accuracy.

Then iterate with users, audit accessibility and prepare browser-store distribution. No improvement percentages are claimed yet.
